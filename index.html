From Bean to Bar

Is a $12 bar of dark chocolate really better than a $4 one? The reviews don't list prices, but they can show what actually makes a good bar. So I went through thousands of them to find out what to look for on the wrapper.

2,841 bars · 58 bean countries · one very busy taster

1The verdict on 2,841 bars

Since 2006, one taster, Brady Brelinski, has rated 2,841 dark chocolate bars on a scale from 1 to 5. A 4 or above means outstanding. Anything under 3 is a disappointment.

You'd expect plenty of great bars. There aren't. Only about 4 in every 100 make it to outstanding, and roughly 1 in 5 lets you down.

2Where do the beans come from?

Cocoa only grows in a narrow strip either side of the equator. I wanted to know if the places that grow the most cocoa also grow the best.

3Who makes the bars?

Cocoa is usually grown in one country and made into chocolate in another. The bars in this data come mostly from small craft makers in the US and Europe, but there are a handful here in Australia too.

Which regions make the most outstanding bars?

Each column is a region where bars are made, and wider columns mean more bars. The dark strip at the top of each column is the share rated outstanding.

Asia, Oceania, Africa and Central America are grouped as "Other regions" because each one is small.

Europe has the biggest outstanding strip, about 5.5% of its bars, against about 4% for North America.

4What's inside the bar?

The wrapper usually tells you two things: how much cocoa is in it, and what else went in. Does either of them matter?

5How do experts describe great chocolate?

For every bar, the taster notes down a few words he remembers about it. Some words keep turning up on the good ones.

If a review says creamy, rich or dried fruit, it's probably a good bar. Bitter and vanilla are warning signs.

A note on the data

All of these ratings come from one person, so in the end they reflect his taste. I think that's actually a strength: every bar was judged the same way, by someone who has tasted thousands of them.

It also means a single bar can move a small country's average a lot. That's why the charts point out when a number is based on only a few bars.

7My chocolate aisle guide

So, is the $12 bar worth it? The reviews don't list prices, so I can't say. But they do show what to look for on the wrapper. Here are four things I'll check next time I'm standing in front of the chocolate shelf.

Aim for 65–79%

At 80% cocoa and above, about 4 in 10 bars disappoint.

Keep it simple

Beans, sugar and cocoa butter. I'd skip bars with added vanilla.

Pick a single origin

Blends (beans mixed from more than one place) averaged 3.05, while single-origin bars averaged 3.21.

Try Pacific beans

Papua New Guinea is the top-rated bean country with at least 20 bars, and it's right next door. The Solomon Islands score even higher, but from only 10 bars.
